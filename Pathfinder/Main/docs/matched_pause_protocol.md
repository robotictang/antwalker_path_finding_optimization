# Matched test of a planned junction pause

Does adding a planned pause improve mission completion over the same thermal speed controller? The [previous benchmark](pause_forecast_confirmation.md) compared speed controllers that used different route rules. The [published speed baseline](published_speed_baseline.md) adapts Trujillo and Cutkosky's gait-period thermal speed rule to the simulated hexapod. Both arms of this experiment use that adaptation. The treatment is whether the route planner can schedule a junction pause.

## Courses and starting states

The [configuration](../experiments/matched_pause.json) fixes 12 synthetic courses, each with two stages, two branch options per stage and a flat intermediate junction. The four alternating courses reverse the sign of the bank on each branch between stages. The four persistent courses preserve the sign on each branch. The four remaining courses have both available branches banked in the same direction on at least one stage. The designs vary turn length, bank length, branch offset, bank angle and the longitudinal position of the slope. They span 37.44 to 40.29 m of projected progress, which is the difference in longitudinal position between the starting point and goal. The geometry spans the earlier test's scale and varies the location of the longer or more similarly banked stage. Each deadline is the projected length divided by 0.108 m/s, rounded to the nearest second. That rate represents the earlier benchmark's deadline scale. No fresh-course outcomes inform these values or select which courses enter the comparison.

Every course uses all three inherited thermal settings with each warm-side assignment, for six matched warm starts per course and 72 in total. The two arms start from the same array of temperatures. Each left-warm/right-warm pair also has the same multiset of temperatures, with the warm and cool sides exchanged. Each arm has the same simulated physics, motor derating, 70 °C thermal stop, mission deadline, two-second planning budget, and 69.50 °C forecast admission ceiling. Both use the same nominal edge profiles collected at 65% and 100% speed. A branch enters the shared route set only when its nominal profile completes at both speeds. All 72 starts remain in the denominator regardless of planning or nominal profile outcomes.

## Paired decision rules

The zero-pause arm forecasts the four geometric routes when all branches are supported. The optional-pause arm can also forecast each route with one 12 s pause at the intermediate junction. Both arms rank admissible candidates by earliest predicted arrival with the same deterministic ranking. A forecast is admissible when its elapsed time is at most the deadline and its peak is strictly below 69.50 °C. In the optional-pause forecast, the robot remains at the intermediate junction for 12 s while its motor temperatures evolve under the previously estimated per-motor hold loads. In physics, the controller commands zero forward speed during the pause while continuing to cycle the feet. The 12 s value and hold loads come from the archived pause study. Fresh-course runs are excluded from their estimation.

The comparison estimates the effect of offering the action to the planner. An optional-pause decision can keep the same route and action, schedule a pause on the same or a different route, or admit a start for which the zero-pause arm found no route. The paired outcome therefore measures the policy effect, including any differences in route choice. Conditional diagnostics on the replays explain which selected pause schedules reached the junction and whether the action was executed.

## Outcomes and analysis

The primary outcome is reaching the goal by the deadline without a simulated thermal stop. Count planning rejections, planning timeouts, thermal stops, deadline misses and other replay failures separately. Report the paired difference in completion rates in percentage points, gains and losses and the difference in the number of thermal stops. The earlier study's practical benchmark applies here as well. It requires at least a 10-percentage-point increase and no additional thermal stops, which means at least eight net added finishes among the 72 starts. A descriptive 95% interval resamples whole courses so that each course's six paired starts remain together. These hand-designed courses do not form a random sample of real terrain.

Report outcomes by course, terrain family, and thermal setting. For the optional arm, count starts with an admissible paused candidate, pauses selected by the planner, selected pauses reached, pauses completed, and thermal stops before and after reaching the junction. Replay every selected paused route with zero pause as a route-preserving diagnostic. Reuse traces for identical warm starts and schedules. Compare the physical trajectories through the sample before the pause. Compare forecast and replay elapsed time and peak temperatures for selected pauses, including junction entry and exit where available. Summarize forecast selection time and any budget exceedances separately from the physical mission time. Freeze the course configuration, method implementation, and archived hold model before starting fresh profiling and warm replays.
