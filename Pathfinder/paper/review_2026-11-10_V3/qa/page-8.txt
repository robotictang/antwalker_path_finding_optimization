6.3 Variation across maps
Figure 2 shows all 31 paired-map relative score  
improvements against PSO-C, ranging from −1.718% 
to 23.202%. Map 24 has the largest positive effect.  
Omitting it changes the mean across the remaining 30 
paired maps from the full-sample mean of 0.690% to  
−0.060%. For the noP comparison, leave-one-map-out 
means  remain  positive  and  range  from  0.682%  to  
0.822%. These post hoc summaries measure influence 
within the unchanged primary sample.
Figure 2. Average over jointly accepted repetitions of 
the CLRP score difference against PSO-C on each 
paired map. Positive values favor CLRP. All points 
remain in the primary mean.
Map  24  remains  in  the  primary  dataset  used  to 
calculate the mean and bootstrap interval. Its influence 
explains the positive mean, alongside the zero median 
and equal numbers of lower-score and higher-score  
pairs  observed  against  PSO-C.  The  omission  
calculation measures concentration  of the result. It  
does  not justify excluding  the map  from the  main  
analysis.
6.4 Selected route comparison
Map  24  belongs  to  the  corridor  family.  Both  
methods accept all three repetitions. CLRP returns the 
same  route  in  every  repetition,  with  a  distance  of 
20.803 m and a score of 1.310. PSO-C has a mean  
distance  of  27.135  m  and  a  mean  score  of  1.706. 
CLRP’s  predicted  time,  nominal  battery  cost  and  
exposure are 32.959 s, 15.446 percentage points and  
1.012, respectively, compared with PSO-C means of  
42.584 s, 20.066 percentage points and 1.665.
For each paired repetition, CLRP has lower values  
in all four components of the score. Figure 3 shows  
repetition zero. PSO-C takes a longer detour to the  
west.  This  explains  why  the  returned  routes  have  
different  scores,  but  does  not  identify  a  specific  
internal factor in the optimizer.
