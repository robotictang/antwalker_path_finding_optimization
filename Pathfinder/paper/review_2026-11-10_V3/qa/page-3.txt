The planner obtains ground-truth position, heading, 
obstacle  geometry  and  terrain  information  directly  
from the simulator. These inputs are not estimated  
from sensor measurements. The start, initial heading,  
destination and estimated return to the charging station 
are held constant across methods for each trial. The  
study therefore compares route choices on the same  
known map. The input model excludes sensor noise,  
localization errors and map reconstruction.
3.2 Route geometry
A path P is a sequence of points on the horizontal  
plane. The first and last points are the specified start  
and destination. The points are connected by straight  
segments in order. The first search grid has a spacing  
of 0.25 m and eight possible directions of motion.  
Diagonal  moves  through  blocked  grid  corners  are  
prohibited.
Every  returned  segment  must  pass  the  same  
clearance test against the planar obstacle geometry.  
The robot radius is set to 1.82 m, the tracking margin  
to 0.12 m and the segment-clearance tolerance to 0.005 
m. These values define the planning clearance model.  
The analysis considers a path for the robot body and  
does not compute individual three-dimensional foot-
placement trajectories.
The  terrain  map  defines  regions  with  different  
walking-speed limits and difficulty values. Equations

Here, the clip function restricts its first argument to

height range in meters, and the population standard  
deviation  of  sampled  ground  heights  in  meters,

sampled at nine positions using offsets of −1.82, 0 and 
1.82  m  along  each  horizontal  axis.  Slope  is  the  
arctangent  of  the  gradient  magnitude  of  the  mean  
sampled height, computed on the 0.25 m grid. For the 
tested terrain grades 0, 1 and 2, f equals 0, 0.25 and  
0.60, respectively. Terrain-grade queries include a 1.1 
m  margin  around  patch  boundaries.  Difficulty  is  
sampled at intervals of no more than 0.125 m along  
each  route  segment.  Each  route  sample  uses  the  
difficulty of its nearest grid cell. Segment exposure is  
the segment length multiplied by its mean sampled  
difficulty.  Total  exposure  R  is  the  sum  across  
segments. This quantity is calculated from the terrain  
map and does not represent an estimated probability of 
slipping.
3.3 Route score and acceptance
The route evaluator calculates distance D in meters, 
predicted outward travel time T in seconds, nominal  
battery  use  E  in  percentage  points,  and  terrain  
exposure  R.  The  initial  heading  is  π/2  radians.  
Predicted  time  includes  travel  at  terrain-dependent  
speed  limits,  heading  changes  at  1  rad/s  and  an  
acceleration allowance. Speed limits for smooth, mild 
and higher-grade terrain are 0.75, 0.65 and 0.38 m/s,  
respectively.
Equation (3) combines the four quantities into a  
score Q. Lower scores are better. The denominators  
express one score unit as 50 m of distance, 90 s of  
predicted time, 30 nominal battery percentage points  
or 80 exposure units. These fixed trade-offs were not  
derived from robot measurements or fitted to the main-
test results.

The  battery  cost  model  assigns  0.70  percentage  
points per walking meter, 0.015 per second, 0.08 per  
turning radian and a 0.10 start/stop allowance. These  
coefficients were not fitted to electrical measurements. 
Distance, travel time and turning also contribute to E,  
so the four score terms are not independent physical  
outcomes.  Combining  them  expresses  a  fixed  
preference  for  these  route  properties.  The  1.25  
multiplier increases the predicted outward-and-return  
cost by 25%, and the six-point reserve adds a fixed  
allowance.  Neither  rule  is  a  statistically  calibrated  
probability  of  completing  a  physical  mission.  All  
evaluations use the same parameters. Sensitivity to  
different score weights or reserve settings is not tested. 
Battery use in this paper means the output of this  
equation-based  model.  Mechanical  joint  work  and  
electrical battery consumption are different quantities; 
the route score does not convert one into the other.
The charging station and return route are fixed once 
per  trial  before  the  planners  are  compared.  Each  
planner then selects an outward route using the same  
return allowance. The destination deadline applies to  
the outward leg. The motion experiment follows the  
outward and return routes and includes eight seconds  
of  simulated  station  service  in  completed  mission  
times.  Whole-mission  distance  and  elapsed  time  
describe execution of the destination-and-return task,  
whereas D and T describe the planned outward route.  
Unfinished  executions  can  end  before  completing  
either leg.
