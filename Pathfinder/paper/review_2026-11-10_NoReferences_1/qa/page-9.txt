7 Discussion
Under the tested settings final local adjustment is  
more  accepted  and  gives  lower  scores  in  more  
accepted attempts than landmark search. Removing it  
results in a loss of 3 acceptances on the corridor maps, 
while removing landmark search results in a loss of  
recorded computation and no loss of acceptance. Rank 
of incumbent route cannot deteriorate in last stage as  
modifications can be allowed only if incentive is the  
retention  of  the  route.  The  empirical  result  is  a  
quantitative estimation of the trade-offs at the chosen  
settings. Separately-timed stage-removal trials do not  
ensure the same history of searches across repetitions. 
Initialization  quality  can  be  separated  from  the  
contribution of later stages using an initial-route-only  
baseline and a matched-budget comparison, both of  
which are not included here. There is no evidence that 
the mean score is higher for CLRP than for PSO-C.  
The mean paired-map relative score improvement is  
0.690%,  the  descriptive  95%  bootstrap  interval  
contains  zero  and  the  median  is  zero.  When  the  
interval is zero, it doesn't mean equality or inferiority. 
There is one more PSO-C attempt and map 24 has  
significant  impact  on  the  positive  mean  relative  
improvement. The mean relative improvements for the 
two  comparisons  (CLRP-versus-PSO-P  and  CLRP-
versus-ACO-P) are positive, and the median paired-
map effects are 0, for the configurations tested. The  
different ways of initialization and the different ways  
of  representation  make  it  impossible  to  credit  
representation  by  itself.  The  five  intervals  are  not  
adjusted  for  multiple  comparisons,  and  are  not  
confirmation evidence. For all methods, every tested  
non-corridor  action  is  accepted  and  therefore,  
differences  in  acceptance  are  limited  to  corridor  
actions in the four map families. The 3 repetitions  
indicate the amount of algorithmic variation within the 
map(s) and do not produce a number of independent  
main-test maps greater than 32. A separate evaluation 
is required for more complex or unknown environs.  
There is no sensitivity evaluation of the score weights 
and  reserve  settings.  There's  an  overlap  of  route  
preferences within the combined score, since distance 
and duration are also factors in battery usage. There is 
no  physical  battery  savings  verified  by  electrical  
measurements  and  the  work  done  by  direct  body  
assistance is not included in the work done by the  
actuators. Search times are not deployment latency  
measurements, but are recorded in the shared-desktop 
configuration,  not  shared-preparation.  The  motion  
diagnostic  defines  dependence  on  direct  body  
assistance  to  the  four  missions  tested.  Completed  
missions  and  low  tilt  do  not  necessarily  prove  a  
walking gait, as assistance is required to correct body  
velocities and roll/pitch rates. That interpretation is  
further curtailed by the six foot contact measurements. 
The scope of the simulation is defined by known map  
and pose inputs, various source and assembled masses, 
and the lack of physical motor, battery, terrain and  
contact measurements. The ability to physically walk  
and sense based navigation is not assessed.
8 Conclusions
Final  local  refinement  improves  the  acceptance  
from 90 to 93 out of 96 attempts on 32 maps for the  
main  test.  In  contrast,  landmark  search  improves  
acceptance  by  0.924%  and  provides  a  conditional  
mean  paired-map  relative  score  improvement  of  
0.924% when compared to its removal, with a mean  
search  time  of 0.196  s  without the  landmarks  and  
0.376 s with the landmarks. This is because the two  
stage-removal  comparisons  exhibit  different  
acceptance-score/computation trade-offs. There is no  
mean score advantage shown over PSO-C: the mean  
paired-map  relative  improvement  is  0.690%,  the  
median is 0, and the description 95% bootstrap interval 
contains 0. PSO-C has one more chance. There are 16 
valid motion executions which each give a separate  
diagnostic. Assistance is given to full scale and half-
scale  to  complete  all  four  missions.  The  zero-
assistance condition was either none or time out at 180 
s,  both  with  and  without  leg-body  gravity  
compensation verified. Under the defined 6-foot test,  
all assisted executions show no foot contact. The study 
thus separates the acceptance of a limited body route  
from the validation of an unassisted locomotion of the 
body. The costs and  tradeoffs reported  are for the  
particular simulation and objective.
