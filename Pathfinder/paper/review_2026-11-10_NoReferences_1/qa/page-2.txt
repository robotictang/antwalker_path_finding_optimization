3 Robot and planning model
3.1 Robot model and body assistance
The robot model has six legs with each leg having  
three articulated joints. The total mass of the imported 
model is 97.52 kg and its base mass is 35 kg. The total 
mass of the assembled MuJoCo model is 113.32 kg  
including the mass of the base 46 kg. It comes with  
wheels and a scanner, for a total of 23 actuators. All  
experiments are performed on foot: Walking mode, the 
wheels and scanner are still considered as part of the  
simulated mass. The MuJoCo robot is shown in the  
walking configuration in Fig. 1, in a static snapshot.  
The  perspective  is  stationary,  allowing  to  see  the  
assembly with the wheels and scanner. Close objects  
are removed from view. This planning is based on the 
maps  and  task  constraints  from  Section  5  and  is  
reflected in the results in Section 6.
Figure 1. Static view of the simulated hexapod in its walking configuration. The wheels and scanner remain part 
of the assembly.
The imported robot spec says that leg-joint torque  
limits are 60 Nm and angular velocity limits are 20  
rad/s. These are specified as limits in the specification. 
No  physical  measurements  of  motor  torque-speed  
characteristics, battery depletion or robot mass. The  
assembled simulation is the one which is evaluated  
here,  while  the  imported  values  for  the  masses  
differentiate  between  source  model  and  assembled  
model. MuJoCo takes 0.004 s steps in advancing the  
physics. The motion controller uses a period of 0.016  
s, or every four physics steps. The acceleration due to  
gravity is 9.81 m/s². Gravity compensation is on by  
default on 18 leg bodies. Gravity compensation and  
direct body assistance are two distinct mechanisms:  
the gravity compensation acts on the selected bodies  
to remove the effect of gravity, and the direct body  
assistance acts on the velocity of the free body. Body  
assistance limits the vertical velocity and roll/pitch  
rates  once  per  controller  frame.  Also  modifies  
horizontal velocity and yaw-rate per physics step. At  
assistance scale 1 the horizontal velocity is set equal to 
the requested velocity. At scale 0.5, each correction  
will keep 50% of the current velocity and add 50% of  
the requested velocity. This mixture is used 4 times in 
each controller frame. These body velocities remain  
the same at scale 0 for the assistance operation. The  
scale is for velocity blending and not for a support  
force.  Ground  truth  position,  heading,  obstacle  
geometry and terrain information is directly accessed  
from the simulator and passed to the planner. These  
inputs are not based on measurement from the sensors.
