Each method is given a search-time limit of 2 s and 
a limit of 240 collision-free routes evaluated.  Route 
evaluations during method initialization  are also part  
of this total. Rejected proposals are collision checked  
(not  complete  route)  and  the  result  counts  as  a  
geometric  query.  A  method  may  stop  below  both  
limits when its search procedures end.  PSO-C may  
take up the evaluation limit before time limit. The wall 
timer  contains  initialization  and  refinement  for  the 
particular method. Shared map preparation takes place 
prior  to  the  methods  running,  and  involves  
preparations of the scenes, construction of common  
graphs,  return-route  preparation  and  calculation  of  
destination-based lower bounds. Reported search time 
thus includes only some planning by all methods, and  
excludes preparation common to all methods for a  
given case.
5 Experimental design
5.1 Maps and task limits
The study uses eight development maps with seeds  
from 2130000 to 2130007 and 32 main-test maps with 
seeds  from  2140000  to  2140031.  The  map  types  
alternate between open, cluttered, rough and corridor. 
There are two development maps and eight main-test  
maps for each category. The horizontal range of each  
map is from −24 to 24 m. Open, Cluttered, Rough and 
Corridor maps have 5, 16, 7 and 4 generated obstacles 
respectively. There are also two interrupted barriers on 
the corridor maps. The four families of maps have 1,  
2, 4 and 3 terrain patches, respectively. Obstacles,  
shallow terrain tiles and areas which affect walking  
speed and exposure are defined by each map. The  
same geometry and terrain is given to all six planners  
for a specific case. Start and destination proposals are  
sampled  between  −17  and  17  m  (with  a  starting  
distance  of  12–26  m)  on  each  axis.  Proposals  are 
moved to free grid locations and kept if their eventual 
separation is ≥ 10 m. Deadlines are 45, 70, 110 and  
160 s. Available nominal battery levels are 45%, 65%, 
85% and 100%, and exposure limits are 10, 20 and 40. 
The  overall  distance  to  the  charging  station  is  
determined in advance prior to comparing the different 
methods. The nominal battery cost coefficients are the 
same  for  all  maps.  Changing  battery  allowances  
changes  the  available  resource  limit  and  not  the  
nominal battery use assigned to a meter or a turn. The 
experiment modifies the geometry of the maps and  
task  constraints.  It  does  not  measure  a  variety  of  
physical battery characteristics.
