PSO-C initializes by adding Gaussian perturbations 
to the internal point coordinates of all particles except 
the first one with a standard deviation of 2 m. Swarm

meters. The particle positions are constrained to the

clearance  assessment  is  rejected.  If  the  particle  
suggestions do not improve the initial optimal path, the 
initial  optimal  path  is  still  available,  and  the  final 
adjustment follows the usual evaluation cut-off.
PSO-C and PSO-P differ in the way of representing 
routes and the way of establishing the initial routes.  
The evaluation looks at the overall effect of these  
implementation choices. It does not control for the  
effect of representation alone. All results displayed are 
relevant  to  the  route  objective  and  the  parameters 
entered.
4.3 Elimination of planning stages and search 
limits
CLRP noL does not include the landmark search  
and  CLRP  noP  does  not  include  the  final  local  
adjustment  or  refinement  .  Both  versions  use  the  
CLRP configuration selected for the stages they keep. 
Specifically, noP removes only the last modification  
and maintains the processes in the original refiner.  
These evaluations measure the effect of deleting a  
particular  phase  from  the  designated  planner.  The  
reduced variants are not re-calibrated on the primary  
test maps. Table 1 shows the selected criteria for the  
search.

Each technique is given a nominal search time of 2  
seconds  and  a  limit  of  240  collision-free  paths  
assessed.  The  collective  threshold  is  supported  by  
route  assessments  in  the  initialization  phase.  
Responses to declined proposals containing collision  
assessments  are  geometric  enquiries  not  full  route  
analyses.  The  technique  could  fall  below  both  
thresholds  when  the  search  processes  are  finished. 
PSO-C is able to exhaust the assessment threshold  
ahead of the time constraint.
The  wall  timer  provides  initialization  and  
enhancement for that method. The preparation of the  
shared map takes place before the execution of the  
methods and consists of the configuration of the scene, 
the construction of the collective graph, the setup of  
the return route and the computation of standard search 
trajectories. The reported search time includes part of  
the  planning  phase,  but  excludes  the  common  
preparatory work for all methods for a particular case.
5 Experimental design
5.1 Maps and task limits
The research employs 8 development maps with  
seeds from 2130000 to 2130007 and 32 main test maps 
with  seeds  from  2140  to  2140031.  The  map  
classifications are alternating between open, cluttered, 
rough and corridor. There are 2 development maps and 
8 main test maps in each category. Each map covers  
−24 to 24 m along each horizontal axis.
The open, disorganized, rugged and corridor maps  
have  5,  16,  7  and  4  generated  impediments  
respectively.  The  corridor  maps  also  feature  two  
discontinuous  barriers.  The  maps  in  these  four  
categories  have  1,  2,  4  and  3  terrain  segments  
respectively. Each map features set obstacles, squares 
of shallow terrain, and areas that affect both movement 
speed and exposure. All six planners are given the  
same geometry and terrain for a given scenario.
We sample proposals for start and destination in the 
range of [−17, 17] m on each axis, keeping an initial  
distance of 12-26 m. The proposals are moved to the  
empty positions in the grid and are kept if the final  
distance is more than 10 meters. The time limits are  
45, 70, 110 and 160 seconds. Nominal battery levels
