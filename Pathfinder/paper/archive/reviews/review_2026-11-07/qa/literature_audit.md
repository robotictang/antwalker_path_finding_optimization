# Literature and reference audit

Reviewed 7 November 2026 (Asia/Kuala_Lumpur), against `Pathfinder-Robot-CLRP-IJ3C-V2.odt` as extracted in `manuscript_paragraphs.txt`. This review does not modify the manuscript. Paragraph identifiers below refer to that extraction.

## Overall finding

All 11 cited items are real and identifiable. No incorrect DOI, author list, volume, issue, page range, or article number was found in the listed references. The Ma, Guo, Yin, Chen, and COCO claims are supported at the level stated in the manuscript. Reference [7]'s **2025 issue year is correct** even though its online-first publication was 19 August 2024. Reference [10]'s 6 November 2026 access date is in the past relative to this review; it is not an error.

The material problems in this part of the paper are imprecise wording and the need to distinguish a citation's support for general methodology from validation of this study's particular implementation. Publisher access failures are recorded below as access limitations, not evidence that a citation is invalid.

## Claim-by-claim checks and suggested wording

### P012: A*, PSO, Ant System, visibility graph — supported, with terminology edits

The A* description is a sound short account of estimated total cost. The PSO summary is supported by the original paper's personal-best and group-best velocity updates. Prefer **“best positions found so far”** to “optimal positions,” which could be read as a claim of known optima. The Ant System description is supported by its pheromone information and greedy heuristic. The visibility-graph description is correct; **“collision-free straight-line segments between visible nodes”** is clearer than “free line segments.” [Hart et al.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/4082128?signout=success), [Kennedy and Eberhart](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150GA/homeworks/hw3/_reading6%201995%20particle%20swarming.pdf), [Dorigo et al.](https://iridia.ulb.ac.be/~mdorigo/Published_papers/All_Dorigo_papers/DorManCol1996tsmcb.pdf), [Lozano-Pérez and Wesley](https://lis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/tlp/collision-free-planning-cacm.pdf).

“Demographic updates” is inappropriate technical wording here. Replace it with **“population updates”** or **“swarm updates.”** The sources support the generic concepts, not equivalence of the manuscript's implementations to the original algorithms. The manuscript already properly limits its global-optimality claim.

### P014: Ma — supported, but one phrase is misleading

The paper addresses least travel-time paths under multiple resource constraints and mode-chain restrictions in a multimodal transport network. It uses A*-based label setting and a multilevel graph. Replace **“under a sequence of resource and mode-sequence constraints”** with **“under multiple resource constraints and mode-chain constraints.”** The sequence restriction concerns modes; “sequence of resource constraints” confuses this distinction. Its network application is correctly separated from planar robot planning. [Publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877042814000676), [author's institutional record](https://liser.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/an-a-label-setting-algorithm-for-multimodal-resource-constrained-/).

### P015: Guo et al. — supported by publisher abstract; full text not verified

The abstract explicitly combines PSO with a control-barrier-function safety velocity adjustment, and reports simulation tests across obstacle scenarios. The manuscript's description is faithful at this level. For precision use **“path planning”** rather than “trajectory planning.” The statement that this manuscript does not reproduce their controller is a claim about the local code and experiment, not something this external article can establish. The source does **not** establish safety guarantees for the present CLRP implementation or justify treating its PSO-C baseline as a reproduction of Guo's method. The current manuscript avoids those claims. [SAGE publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01423312241264860).

### P016: Yin et al. — supported

The article identifies three modules: swing-leg trajectories, gait period/duty factor, and gait sequence, and performs experiments on LittleStrong. The manuscript's “swing trajectories, gait timing and gait sequence” is accurate. Prefer the specific **“gait period and duty factor”** over “timing.” Do not transfer its measured energy results to this study's nominal battery model. [Springer full text, abstract and §3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10033-024-01000-0).

### P016: Chen et al. — supported, but use the actual technical terms

The paper constructs terrain-dependent traversal and foothold costs, improves A*, plans a free gait, and reports simulation and physical robot validation. The manuscript's distinction from its own route-level experiment is appropriate. Replace **“topographical characteristics with travel expenses, foot positioning and movement strategies”** with **“geometric and physical terrain features with traversal-cost planning, foothold selection, and free-gait planning.”** “Travel expenses” sounds financial, and “topographical” alone omits the physical properties. [Frontiers full text, abstract and §§3–4](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2025.1605938/full).

### P017: COCO — supported as methodological inspiration

The official procedure discusses initialization, fixed parameter procedures, disclosure of tuning, termination, evaluation counts, and reporting the computation environment. Its central search-cost measure is calls to the objective, and it calls for a separate timing experiment. This supports transparent reporting here. It does **not** make this terrain-aware robotics comparison a COCO benchmark or certify that equal nominal caps yield equal work. Keep **“informed by COCO's reporting principles”** if revising this sentence. The paper's distinction between full route evaluations, geometric queries, and wall time is reasonable, but is this study's adaptation. [Official COCO procedure, Conducting the Experiment; Parameter Setting; Time Complexity Experiment](https://numbbo.github.io/coco-doc/experimental-setup/).

### P104: Efron — valid foundational citation; method details belong to this study

Efron [11] introduces bootstrap inference. It is a suitable foundational reference, but does not by itself validate this study's map sampling, conditioning on mutual acceptance, 5,000 resamples, seed, percentile construction, or unadjusted multiple comparisons. Those are author decisions. Suggested wording: **“Using the bootstrap principle [11], we resample paired maps with replacement 5,000 times and report the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resampled mean.”** Confirm that the saved code performs exactly that calculation before using the sentence. The current explicit conditional/descriptive limitations should remain. [Efron DOI/publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344552).

## Additional internal inconsistency noticed

P102 states that a positive relative difference means CLRP is worse, whereas P208/P209/P214 and the reported interpretation say positive values favor CLRP. This is a substantive sign-description conflict independent of the bibliography. The equation object was not present in the paragraph extraction, so compare Equation (5) to the actual analysis code before changing it. If the formula is `(Qc − Qv) / Qc × 100`, then positive values mean CLRP has the lower score and is better.
