Body  assistance  limits  vertical  movement  and  
roll/pitch  rates  once  every  controller  cycle.  It  also 
changes the horizontal velocity and the yaw rate with  
each iteration of the physics. At assistance level 1 the  
horizontal speed is adjusted to the desired speed. An  
adjustment at scale 0.5 maintains 50% of the current  
speed while adding 50% of the desired speed. The  
mixture is used four times per controller frame. The  
support  operation  at  scale  0  preserves  these  body  
velocities. The scale is a measure of the amalgamation 
of velocities, not a force of support.
The planner obtains the exact position, orientation,  
obstacle dimensions and terrain information directly  
from the simulator. These inputs are not based on  
sensor  readings.  All  methods  start  from  the  same  
starting point, in the same main direction, to the same  
end point and estimated return to the charging station  
in each trial. The study therefore looks at route choices 
on an identical, known map. Sensor noise, localisation 
errors and map reconstruction are not considered by  
the input model.
3.2 Route geometry
A trajectory P is a sequence of points located on the 
horizontal plane. The start and end points indicate the  
specified starting and ending locations. The points are 
connected  by  straight  segments  in  order.  The  first 
search grid has a separation of 0.25 meters and eight  
possible directions to go. No diagonal moves through  
blocked grid corners allowed.
Every  returned  segment  must  undergo  the  same 
clearance  analysis  in  accordance  with  the  planar  
obstacle configuration. The robot’s radius is set to 1.82 
m, the tracking margin is set to 0.12 m and the segment 
clearance tolerance is set to 0.005 m. These values  
provide the framework for planning clearance. The  
analysis considers only a path for the robot body and  
does not compute distinct 3D foot-placement paths.

3.3 Route score and acceptance
The route assessor calculates distance D (in meters), 
estimated outward travel time T (in seconds), normal  
battery  usage  E  (in  percentage  points)  and  terrain  
exposure R. The title is in radians (π/2). The expected 
time  includes  transit  at  speed  limits  imposed  by  
terrain, turns at 1 rad/s, and allowance for acceleration. 
The speed limits for flat, gentle and steep terrain are  
0.75, 0.65 and 0.38 m/s respectively.
Equation  (3)  integrates  the  four  variables  into  a 
single score Q. Lower scores are preferred. The values 
are  representative  of  one  score  unit  in  50  meters  
distance, in 90 seconds expected time, in 30 nominal  
battery percentage points, or in 80 exposure units.  
They were fixed trade-offs for the experiment and  
were  not  derived  from  robotic  measurements  or  
adjusted to the main test results.

The  battery  cost  model  assigns  0.70  percentage  
points per metre walked, 0.015 per second, 0.08 per  
radian turned and an allowance of 0.10 for starting and 
stopping.  These  coefficients  were  not  fitted  to  
electrical measurements. E is also influenced by the  
distance, travel time and turning, so the four scoring  
components are not independent physical outcomes.

there is a bias toward these route features. The 1.25  
multiplier increases the expected cost for the outward  
and return by 25%. The six-point reserve adds a fixed 
allowance. Neither guideline is a statistically validated
