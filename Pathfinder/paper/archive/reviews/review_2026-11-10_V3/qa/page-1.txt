Constrained Route Planning for a Simulated Hexapod
Jin Wayne Foo
waynejinfoo123@gmail.com
Abstract
In this work, we evaluate landmark search and final 
local  modification  in  body-route  planning  for  a  
simulated hexapod. Six configurations share obstacle-
clearance,  deadline,  nominal  battery  and  terrain-
exposure constraints. Parameter selection consists of  
64 trials over eight development maps; the main test  
consists  of  576  trials  over  32  maps,  giving  640  
planning  trials.  The  complete  planner,  Constrained 
Landmark Route Planning (CLRP), accepts 93 of 96  
main-test attempts, compared with 94 for coordinate-
based particle swarm optimization, PSO-C. Removing 
final adjustment reduces acceptance to 90. Without  
landmark  search,  93  acceptances  are  retained  and  
mean  search  time  falls  from  0.376  to  0.196  s.  
Complete CLRP has a conditional mean paired-map  
relative  score  improvement  of  0.924%  over  this  
reduced version. Against PSO-C, the mean paired-map 
relative score improvement is 0.690%, with a zero  
median and a descriptive 95% bootstrap interval of  
[−0.255, 2.312]%. This interval includes zero and does 
not demonstrate a mean score advantage. A separate  
diagnostic consists of 16 executions: four missions  
under four assistance conditions. Full and half-scale  
assistance each complete all four missions. Both zero-
assistance conditions complete none and time out at  
180  s.  Assisted  executions  record  no  foot  contact  
under the six-foot contact test. The results quantify  
planning trade-offs and dependence on direct body  
assistance. Nominal battery costs are model outputs  
without physical or electrical validation.
Keywords: Hexapod;  planning;  optimization;  
simulation; reproducibility
1 Introduction
A robot's path must avoid obstacles and satisfy the  
conditions of its task. A short route can still miss a  
deadline or leave insufficient battery reserve to reach  
a charging station. The current model accounts for  
terrain-dependent  walking  speeds,  the  time  and  
nominal battery use associated with turns, and limits  
on exposure to difficult terrain. Route selection must  
account for these costs and separately verify that all  
task requirements are satisfied.
This  study  investigates  the  impact  of  landmark  
search and final local route adjustments on acceptance, 
route score and computation in MuJoCo [1], while  
controlling for map, evaluator and task constraints.  
The  full  planner,  CLRP,  includes  initial  corridor  
search, additional links around obstacles and terrain  
boundaries, and local refinement of intermediate route 
coordinates. The analysis includes particle swarm and 
ant-inspired planners that choose points from initial  
routes,  a  particle  swarm  planner  that  changes  the  
coordinates of intermediate points, and two versions of 
CLRP with one search stage removed. The experiment 
evaluates the implemented configurations; it does not  
propose  a  new  swarm  update  rule  or  establish  
universal planner superiority.
The study distinguishes three outcomes: acceptance 
under resource constraints, score differences among  
attempts  accepted  by  both  methods,  and  the  
computation  needed  to  find  routes.  We  use  eight  
development maps to select configurations before the  
main  evaluation  on  32  maps  with  three  paired  
repetitions.  Stage-removal  comparisons  assess  
whether additional search improves an accepted route 
or changes acceptance. Four separate maps are used to 
examine  reliance  on  direct  body  assistance,  
distinguishing  route  acceptance  from  validation  of  
locomotion.
The  hexapod  application  determines  the  two-
dimensional  body-clearance  envelope  and  the  
assumptions about walking speed and turning. The  
planner receives the map and pose directly and plans  
body trajectories rather than full sequences of foot  
placements.  The  results  apply  to  the  specified  
geometric  and  nominal  resource  model.  This  
framework  allows  consistent  evaluation  of  the  
planning stages. Nominal battery costs and assisted  
movement are not measurements of physical robot  
performance.
2 Related Literature
A* explores a graph using the sum of the cost to  
reach a point and an estimate of the remaining cost [2]. 
Particle  swarm  optimization  updates  candidate  
solutions using particle motion and the best positions  
found by individual particles and the swarm [3]. The  
ant system uses pheromone and heuristic information  
to  guide  route  construction  [4].  A  visibility  graph  
connects points with unobstructed straight segments  
[5].  These  established  methods  inform  the  initial  
search, population updates and graph connections used 
here. The constrained search keeps its best candidate,  
but that candidate is not necessarily globally optimal.
Routing under resource constraints involves more  
than minimizing the length of one route. Ma integrates 
A*  and  label  setting  for  multimodal  routes  under  
resource  and  mode-sequence  constraints  [6].  The  
transportation  model  describes  related  constrained  
search techniques, but its network and mode-chain  
problems differ from the planar robot paths studied  
here. In the present method, a finite set of initial search 
labels  and  landmark  candidates  is  followed  by  
continuous  point  refinement.  A  single  evaluator  
assesses deadlines, nominal battery allowances for the 
outward and return routes, and terrain exposure.
