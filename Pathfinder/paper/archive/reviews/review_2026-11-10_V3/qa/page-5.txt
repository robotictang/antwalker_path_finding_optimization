Each method receives a nominal search-time limit  
of 2 s and a limit of 240 evaluated collision-free routes. 
Route evaluations during initialization count toward  
this  shared  limit.  Collision  checks  on  rejected  
proposals  count  as  geometric  queries  rather  than  
complete route evaluations. A method can stop below 
both limits when its search procedures finish. PSO-C  
can exhaust the evaluation limit before the time limit.
The  wall  timer  includes  initialization  and  
refinement  for  the  individual  method.  Shared  map 
preparation  occurs  before  the  methods  run  and  
includes  scene  setup,  common  graph  construction,  
return-route preparation and calculation of nominal  
search  directions.  Reported  search  time  therefore  
covers  part  of  the  planning  process  and  excludes  
preparation shared by all methods for a given case.
5 Experimental design
5.1 Maps and task limits
The study uses eight development maps with seeds  
from 2130000 to 2130007 and 32 main-test maps with 
seeds from 2140000 to 2140031. The map categories  
alternate between open, cluttered, rough and corridor.  
Each category has two development maps and eight  
main-test maps. Each map covers −24 to 24 m along  
both horizontal axes.
Open, cluttered, rough and corridor maps contain 5, 
16, 7 and 4 generated obstacles, respectively. Corridor 
maps also contain two interrupted barriers. Maps in  
these four families contain 1, 2, 4 and 3 terrain patches, 
respectively.  Each  map  defines  obstacles,  shallow  
terrain tiles and regions that determine walking speed  
and  exposure.  All  six  planners  receive  the  same  
geometry and terrain for a given case.
Start and destination proposals are sampled within  
[−17, 17] m on each axis with an initial separation of  
12–26 m. Proposals are moved to free grid locations  
and retained if their final separation is at least 10 m.  
Deadlines  are  45,  70,  110  and  160  s.  Available  
nominal battery levels are 45%, 65%, 85% and 100%, 
and exposure limits are 10, 20 and 40. The return route 
to the charging station is estimated before the methods 
are compared.
The nominal battery cost coefficients are identical  
for all maps. Changing battery allowances changes the 
available resource limit, not the nominal battery use  
assigned to a meter or a turn. The experiment changes 
map geometry and task constraints. It does not test a  
calibrated range of physical battery properties.
5.2 Parameter selection and repeated tests
Each of the four base methods is tested with two  
parameter  profiles  on  the  development  maps.  
Selection first favors the profile with more accepted  
attempts. If acceptance counts are equal, the profile  
with the lower mean score among accepted attempts is 
selected. Exact ties retain the default profile. Every  
base  method  selects  the  default,  and  both  reduced 
CLRP variants inherit that selection.
