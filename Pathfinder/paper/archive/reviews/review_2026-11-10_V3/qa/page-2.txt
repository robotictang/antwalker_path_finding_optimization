Guo et al. [7] propose a hybrid approach to mobile-
robot  trajectory  planning  based  on  particle  swarm  
optimization  and  control-barrier-function  velocity  
adjustments. Their safety mechanism ties planning to  
speed  control.  The  current  comparison  retains  the  
segment-clearance test and compares acceptance and  
score under the same resource constraints. It does not  
implement or evaluate their controller. Changing the  
collision  model,  initialization  or  execution  method  
affects both the comparison and the optimizer.
Hexapod research also addresses motion below the  
body-route  level.  Yin  et  al.  optimize  swing  
trajectories,  gait  timing  and  gait  sequence  on  
LittleStrong [8]. Chen et al. integrate terrain features  
with traversal costs, foot placement and locomotion  
strategy in simulation and physical experiments [9].  
The  current  work  holds  the  gait  controller  fixed,  
supplies  terrain  data  directly  and  investigates  the  
removal of body-route planning stages. Its separate  
assistance diagnostic does not replace the physical and 
foot-placement assessments in those studies.
These  studies  lead  to  a  specific  experimental  
question: how do acceptance and route score change  
when landmark search and final adjustment are added 
under a common evaluator? Complete configurations  
are compared because their initialization procedures  
and route representations differ. They are not replicas  
of every referenced planner. The research documents  
these choices and counts both route evaluations and  
geometric queries, following the COCO procedure’s  
emphasis  on  initialization,  parameter  selection  and 
termination criteria [10]. The comparison applies to  
the specified implementations and resource model.
3 Robot and planning model
3.1 Robot model and body assistance
The  robot  model  has  six  legs,  each  with  three  
articulated joints. The imported model has a total mass 
of 97.52 kg and a base mass of 35 kg. The assembled  
MuJoCo model has a total robot mass of 113.32 kg and 
a base mass of 46 kg. The assembly includes wheels  
and  a  scanner,  giving  23  actuators  in  total.  All  
experiments  use  walking  mode;  the  wheels  and  
scanner remain part of the simulated mass.
Figure 1 shows the MuJoCo robot in a static view  
of  its  walking  configuration.  The  fixed  viewpoint  
shows the assembly, including the wheels and scanner. 
Nearby obstacles are hidden for clarity. The planning  
results in Section 6 use the maps and task constraints  
described in Section 5.

The  imported  robot  specification  lists  leg-joint  
limits of 60 Nm torque and 20 rad/s angular velocity.  
These  are  declared  limits  in  the  specification.  The 
study includes no physical measurements of motor  
torque-speed characteristics, battery depletion or robot 
mass.  The  assembled  simulation  is  the  platform  
evaluated here; the imported mass values distinguish  
the source model from the assembled model.
MuJoCo advances the physics in 0.004 s steps. The 
motion controller runs once every four physics steps,  
or every 0.016 s. Gravitational acceleration is 9.81  
m/s².  In  the  standard  configuration,  gravity  
compensation is enabled on 18 leg bodies. Gravity  
compensation and direct body assistance are separate  
mechanisms: the former offsets gravity on selected  
bodies,  while  the  latter  directly  changes  the  free  
body’s velocity.
Body  assistance  constrains  vertical  motion  and  
roll/pitch  rates  once  per  controller  frame.  It  also  
changes  horizontal  velocity  and  yaw  rate  at  each  
physics step. At assistance scale 1, horizontal velocity 
is set to the requested velocity. At scale 0.5, each  
correction retains 50% of the current velocity and adds 
50% of the requested velocity. This blend is applied  
four  times  per  controller  frame.  At  scale  0,  the  
assistance  operation  leaves  these  body  velocities  
unchanged.  The  scale  specifies  velocity  blending  
rather than a support force.
