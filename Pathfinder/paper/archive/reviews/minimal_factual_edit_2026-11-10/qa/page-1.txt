Abstract
In this work we assess landmark search and final  
local  modification  in  body-route  planning  for  a  
simulated  hexapod.  Obstacle-clearance,  deadline,  
nominal battery and terrain-exposure constraints are  
shared  by  six  configurations.  Parameter  selection  
includes 64 trials and 8 development maps, the main  
test includes 576 trials and 32 maps, resulting in 640  
planning  trials.  The  complete  planner,  Constrained 
Landmark  Route  Planning  (CLRP)  achieves  
acceptance of 93/96 main-test attempts as compared to 
94/96  for  coordinate-based  particle  swarm  
optimization  (PSO-C)  and  90/96  for  the  planner  
without final adjustment. Removing landmark search 
retains 93 acceptances and reduces mean search time  
from  0.376  to  0.196  s.  Complete  CLRP  has  a  
conditional  mean  paired-map  relative  score  
improvement of 0.924% over this reduced version.  
The  mean  paired-map  relative  score  improvement  
against PSO-C is 0.690%, the zero median and the  
descriptive 95 % bootstrap interval is [−0.255, 2.312]  
%. This statistical interval contains zero and does not  
demonstrate  a  mean  score  advantage.  A  separate 
diagnostic  comprises  16  executions :  four  missions 
under four assistance conditions.  Full and half-scale  
assistance each finish all four missions.  Both zero-
assistance conditions complete none and time out at  
180  s. Assisted  executions  have  no  detected  foot  
contact under the six-foot criterion.  Results quantify  
the planning tradeoffs and dependence on direct body  
assistance.  The  nominal  battery  costs  are  model  
outputs  which  are  not  physically  or  electrically  
validated.
Keywords: Hexapod;  planning;  optimization;  
simulation; reproducibility
1 Introduction
The movement path  of a robot should not have  
obstacles and meet the conditions of the task. Even a 
short route can miss its deadline or leave insufficient  
battery reserve to reach a charging station. The current 
model considers walking speed in different terrain,  
time of turns and nominal battery use, and exposure to 
difficult  terrain.  These  costs  must  be  taken  into  
consideration when selecting the route, and should be  
determined  separately  whether  all  the  task  
requirements  are  met.  In  this  work,  we  want  to  
examine how landmark search and the resulting final 
local  route  refinement  during  planning  affects  
acceptance, route score and computation in MuJoCo  
[1]  whilst  controlling  for  map,  evaluator  and  task  
constraints.  The  complete  planner  (CLRP)  features 
initial corridor search, more links around obstacles and 
terrain  boundaries,  and  local  refinement  of  
intermediate  route  coordinates.  The  two  particle  
swarm  and  ant-inspired  planners  which  select  the  
points  from  the  initial  routes,  the  particle  swarm  
planner  which  modifies  the  coordinates  of  the  
intermediate points, and the two versions of CLRP  
with the elimination of one search stage are analyzed.  
The  experiment  is  not  intended  to  provide  a  new  
swarm update rule but to assess the configurations  
used.  The  study  contrasts  between  three  goals:  
accepting  with  resource  limitation,  differences  in  
scores of accepted attempts for both approaches, and  
the  number  of  computations  required  to  determine 
routes.  Eight  development  maps  are  used  to  pick  
configurations prior to  the major evaluation on 32  
maps  with  three  paired  repetitions.  Stage-removal  
comparisons are used to determine if adding more  
search will make an accepted route better or change  
the level of acceptance. There are four separate maps  
that investigate dependence on direct body support,  
separating  route  acceptance  from  locomotion  
validation.  The  assumptions  of  walking  speed  and 
turning,  along  with  the  two-dimensional  body-
clearance  envelope  depend  on  the  hexapod  
application. The map and pose are given directly to the 
planner which then plans body trajectories and not  
complete sequences of foot placements. The results are 
for  the  geometry  and  nominal  resource  model  
specified. This is a consistent framework to evaluate  
the  planning  stages.  Battery  cost  and  assisted  
movement is  not a measurement of physical robot  
performance.
2 Related Literature
A* uses the sum of the cost incurred to reach a point 
and a heuristic estimate of the remaining cost [2]. The 
particle  swarm  optimization  updates  the  candidate  
solutions by using particle motion and the best position 
of each particle and the swarm [3]. The ant system  
bases on pheromone and heuristic information to guide 
route construction [4]. A visibility graph is a graph that 
links  points  by  straight  line  segments  that  are  
unobstructed  [5].  The  initial  search,  population  
updates  and  graph  connections  employed  here  are  
based on these proven methods. The best candidate  
found by the constrained search is not guaranteed to be 
the  best  candidate  in  the  world.  Under  resource  
constraints, routing is more than just minimizing the  
length one route. Ma combines A* and label setting  
for multimodal routes with consideration for resources 
and mode-sequences [6]. Although the transportation  
model describes related constrained search techniques, 
the network and mode-chain problems considered here 
are different than the planar robot paths. The current  
approach is based on an initial search label set and a  
set of landmark candidates to which continuous point  
refinement  is  applied.  Deadlines,  nominal  battery
