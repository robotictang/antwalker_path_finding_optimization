Constrained Route Planning for a Simulated Hexapod
Jin Wayne Foo
waynejinfoo123@gmail.com
Abstract
In this work, we evaluate landmark identification  
and final local modification in body route navigation  
for a simulated hexapod . There are six configurations 
with the same obstacle clearance, deadline, nominal  
battery  and  terrain  exposure  parameters.  The  
parameter  selection  consisted  of  64  trials  over  8  
development maps. The main test consisted of 576  
trials over 32 maps, for a total of 640 planning trials.  
The  comprehensive  planner  Constrained  Landmark 
Route  Planning  (CLRP)  succeeds  in  93  out  of  96 
primary test attempts, compared to 94 for coordinate-
based particle swarm optimization, PSO-C. If the last  
adjustment is removed, acceptance falls to 90. Without 
landmark search, 93 acceptances are retained and the  
average search duration is reduced from 0.376 seconds 
to  0.196  seconds.  The  full  CLRP  results  in  a  
conditional mean paired-map relative score increase of 
0.924% compared to this short version. The paired  
map had an average relative score improvement of  
0.690% over PSO-C, with a median of zero and a 95% 
bootstrap  confidence  interval  of  [−0.255,  2.312]%. 
This range includes zero and does not indicate a better 
average score. A separate diagnostic consists of 16  
runs: four tasks in four support conditions. All four  
objectives  are  met  by  comprehensive  and  partial  
support Zero-assistance conditions do not complete  
and time out at 180 seconds. Assisted executions show 
no foot contact in the six foot contact assessment. The 
results  quantify  the  tradeoffs  in  planning  and  
dependence on direct physical support. The nominal  
battery  costs  are  results  of  models  not  validated  
physically or electrically.
Keywords: Hexapod;  strategising;  optimization;  
simulation; reproducibility
1 Introduction
A robot's path must avoid obstacles and satisfy the  
conditions of its task. A short route can still miss a  
deadline or have insufficient battery capacity to reach  
a  charging  station.  The  current  model  takes  into  
account the fact that the terrain can affect the allowed  
walking speed, that turns can increase the time and  
nominal battery usage and that the encounters with  
difficult  terrain  are  limited.  Taking  into  account  
expenses  in  route  selection  and  independently  
verifying all task requirements.
This  study  investigates  the  impact  of  landmark  
search and final local route adjustments on acceptance, 
route  evaluation,  and  computation  in  MuJoCo  [1], 
while  controlling  for  map,  evaluator,  and  task  
limitations. The full planner, CLRP includes initial  
corridor search, additional links around obstacles and  
terrain  edges, and local refinement of  intermediate  
route  coordinates.  The  analysis  includes  a  particle  
swarm  and  an  ant-inspired  planner  which  choose  
points  from  initial  trajectories,  a  particle  swarm  
planner which changes the coordinates of intermediate 
points, and two versions of CLRP with one search  
phase removed. The experiment is an evaluation of the 
existing configurations and not a proposal of a new  
swarm update rule or a planner that is always better.
The study found 3 results, acceptance within the  
limits of resources, variation of the score based on  
mutual  acceptance  through  both  methods  and  the  
calculations needed to calculate the routes. We use  
eight  development  maps  for  configuration  
determination before the main evaluation on 32 maps  
with  three  paired  iterations.  Stage-removal  
comparisons  assess  if  supplementary  searches  
improve an approved route or change its acceptance.  
Four different maps are used to examine reliance on  
direct bodily support, differentiating between  route  
acceptance and verification of locomotion.
The  assumptions  about  walking  velocity,  and  
rotation,  and  the  two-dimensional  body-clearance  
envelope are determined by the hexapod application.  
The planner directly receives the map and pose and  
plans body trajectories instead of full sequences of foot 
placements. The results are in accordance with the  
prescribed geometric and nominal resource regime.  
This framework allows a consistent evaluation of the  
planning  steps.  Nominal  battery  costs  and  assisted 
movement  are  not  measures  of  actual  robot  
performance.
2 Related Literature
A* explores a graph based on the sum of the cost to 
reach a point and an estimate of the cost remaining [2]. 
Particle  swarm  optimisation  improves  possible  
solutions by leveraging the movement of particles, and 
the optimal positions found by individual particles and 
the  collective  swarm  [3].  The  ant  system  uses  
pheromonal  and  heuristic  information  to  guide  the 
formation of routes [4]. A visibility graph connects  
points by free line segments [5]. These established  
methods inform the preliminary search, demographic  
updates, and graph interconnections used here. The  
restricted search keeps its best candidate, but it is not  
necessarily globally optimal.
